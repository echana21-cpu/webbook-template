01. Matt 5:3 The Blessedness of the Poor in Spirit — Possessing the Kingdom of Heaven Now

  • Passage: Matthew 5:3
  • Title: The Blessedness of the Poor in Spirit — Possessing the Kingdom of Heaven Now

[Introduction] The First Beatitude — The Subversion of Blessing and the Radical Proclamation of the “Kingdom of Heaven” (Matt 5:3)

Beloved saints, beginning today, we will examine step by step the most radiant and magnificent teaching proclaimed by Jesus on the hills of Galilee, namely The Beatitudes, which opens the Sermon on the Mount.

When Jesus saw the crowds, went up on the mountain, and opened His mouth to teach them, what was the very first word that burst forth?

It was the astonishing proclamation: ”Blessed (μακάριοι)”:

Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.”* (Matt 5:3)

People of the world traditionally speak of the ”Five Blessings (O-bok)”: longevity, wealth, health and peace of body and mind, practicing virtue toward others, and passing away peacefully at a ripe old age.

The worldly definition of blessing constantly shifts depending on circumstance and condition. A fashion model is expected to be tall, yet excessive height brings everyday inconvenience; a Japanese sumo wrestler is considered blessed when heavy. Worldly blessing is invariably relative and bound to external conditions.

Yet dear saints, Jesus did not come into this world merely to add eight new beatitudes on top of the world’s five blessings to offer thirteen blessings or a larger quantity of worldly gains.

The Lord came to overturn the world’s concept of blessing at its root, presenting a completely new list of blessings to be enjoyed by the citizens of the Kingdom of God.

The blessing (‘makarioi’, μακάριοι) proclaimed by the Lord here does not denote accidental good luck or fleeting emotional happiness. It signifies an objective, holy spiritual status recognized and guaranteed by the living God, wholly independent of worldly conditions.

And at the very forefront of this new list of blessings, Jesus placed an extraordinary proclamation. That is none other than the ‘Kingdom of Heaven’!

Observe the architecture of the Beatitudes. The first beatitude (v. 3) begins with ”for theirs is the kingdom of heaven,” and the eighth beatitude (v. 10) concludes with ”for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.”

The opening gateway of Christian faith is the Kingdom of Heaven, and the ultimate consummation of discipleship is likewise the Kingdom of Heaven. Today, our Lord powerfully summons us to possess this eternal Kingdom.

[Point 1] The Theological Reality of ‘Poverty in Spirit (πτωχός)’ — The Confession of Spiritual Bankruptcy

(1) The Distinction Between ‘Ptochos (πτωχός)’ and ‘Penes (πένης)’

Who then is able to possess this glorious Kingdom of Heaven?

The Lord sets forth a single condition:

”The poor in spirit (οἱ πτωχοὶ τῷ πνεύματι).”

When we hear the phrase ‘poor in spirit,’ we easily mistake it for mere moral modesty, a gentle disposition, or a passive mindset of self-deprecation.

Yet dear saints, are there not countless people in the secular world who do not attend church or believe in Jesus, yet possess refined manners and humble demeanors? Do they thereby possess the Kingdom of Heaven? By no means.

In Biblical Greek, there are two distinct words denoting poverty:

  1. Penes (πένης): The poverty of a day laborer who lives day-to-day, a relative poverty where one can somehow survive on one’s own by tightening the belt.
  2. Ptochos (πτωχός): The word employed in our text today, designating ‘absolute destitution’, namely the condition of a ‘beggar’ who cannot survive for a single moment on his own and must rely wholly upon the mercy and aid of another.

The ‘poverty in spirit’ spoken of by Jesus is precisely this πτωχός (ptochos)!

It is not a polite self-reflection such as, “Lord, I am somewhat lacking morally.”

Rather, it is the desperate confession of complete spiritual bankruptcy:

“Lord, before You I am an absolute spiritual beggar and a bankrupt soul. There is nothing righteous within me, and if Your grace and mercy are not poured out, I cannot breathe spiritually for even a single second!”

(2) The Spiritual Squandering of ‘Those Rich in Heart’

Yet what is the actual reality of countless people today? Tragically, the majority live as ‘rich in heart.’

To be poor in money means having no financial resources when one wishes to spend.

To be poor in knowledge means being illiterate, unable to read the words one wishes to understand.

What then does it mean to be poor in heart? It means ‘having no remaining capacity or reserves of heart to spend.’

Look at people in the world. They spend their entire heart on making money. They exhaust their hearts to gain promotion and survive competition in the workplace. They pour out their whole soul into children’s anxieties, entertainment, and secular ambitions.

Because they have so many avenues in which to invest their desires, they live as ‘the rich in heart,’ spending lavishly on worldly concerns.

When they return home thoroughly depleted after spending their entire heart at work all day, they have no heart left to converse with family, nor any heart to pray.

When they come before the holy God to worship, they collapse in spiritual numbness, saying, “Lord, I am too exhausted; I have no room in my heart to offer You.”

This is not poverty of spirit. It is a wretched state of squandering the soul in worldly wealth while remaining spiritually paralyzed toward God.

[Point 2] Declaring ‘Default’ Toward the World and Mary’s Broken Alabaster Flask

(1) The Resolution of Default: Having No Heart Left for the World

Dear saints, what is the holy ‘poverty of spirit’ spoken of by our Lord?

It means ‘having no heart left to pay toward the affairs of the world!’

Even though money, time, and physical energy exist in our daily lives, regarding worldly anxieties, secular boasts, and fleeting greed, we move beyond a mere ‘moratorium’ and declare complete ‘default.’

  • A moratorium is an extension request: “My circumstances are difficult now, but give me time and I will pay later.”
  • A default is an absolute declaration of bankruptcy: “I have no capacity to pay you, nor any intention to pay. I am completely bankrupt!”

When the world constantly presents its invoices, demanding, “Worry about money, tremble over your children’s future, invest your soul in worldly success,” the one who is poor in spirit boldly proclaims:

“World, I am sorry, but I do not have a single penny of heart left to pay you. My entire heart is already fully reserved for the living God alone!”

(2) The Total Devotion of Mary Breaking Her Alabaster Flask

Do you recall Mary of Bethany in the Gospels?

Mary approached Jesus carrying an alabaster flask of extremely costly ointment, worth a full year’s wages for a laborer.

She could have simply unsealed the cap, poured a small portion upon Jesus’ feet, and preserved the rest. Yet what did she do? She broke the entire flask and poured it all upon the Lord’s feet without reservation.

Why did she break the flask?

It was a holy declaration of severance: “I will never use this precious perfume for any other purpose in my life.” She completely severed all other worldly possibilities, devoting her absolute all solely unto the Lord.

This is the true portrait of a poor spirit sought by Jesus.

Consider the Apostle Paul. When he stood before Roman Governor Festus and King Agrippa as a prisoner bound in chains, Paul cried out boldly:

Whether short or long, I would to God that not only you but also all who hear me this day might become such as I am—except for these chains.”* (Acts 26:29)

Even before rulers wielding dazzling worldly power, Paul did not shrink back. He paid no heart to worldly honor, wealth, or authority, because his heart was anchored exclusively to the throne of heaven and the grace of the cross.

[Conclusion] Possessing the Kingdom of Heaven in the Present Tense and God’s Absolute Reign

(1) Not Merely an Afterlife Destination, but ‘The Kingdom Possessed Now’

Beloved saints, what is the blessing promised to those who declare default toward the world and pour out their whole heart upon God alone?

”For theirs is the kingdom of heaven!”

We commonly think of the Kingdom of Heaven only as a distant future realm to be entered after physical breath ceases.

Certainly, it is an immutable truth that believers enter eternal heaven upon departing this earth.

Yet when we read Matthew 5:3 in the original text, it does not say in the future tense that “the kingdom of heaven shall be theirs.” Rather, it is a clear present tense: ”theirs is the kingdom of heaven (ἐστὶν αὐτῶν).”

Jesus came to this earth directly from heaven.

From the vantage point of the Lord who descended from heaven, what great blessing could longevity or worldly riches possibly be?

The Lord brought the glorious life and rest of the heavenly throne, the Kingdom of Heaven itself, as a present gift into the midst of our daily existence.

It is a life that does not remain confined to bios (βίος), mere biological survival, but is empowered by zoe (ζωή), God’s eternal divine life, enjoying the holy reign of the Kingdom of God today in our homes, workplaces, and daily lives.

(2) The Miracle When the Heart Is Surrendered to God

Dear saints, who is the true Billionaire rich toward all creation?

It is our Heavenly Father, the Sovereign Owner of the universe, who did not spare even His only begotten Son Jesus Christ on the cross to redeem us!

When we empty our hearts and become poor regarding worldly anxieties and desires, the living Heavenly Father enters and takes His throne within that empty heart.

When we cease paying our stubbornness and worry to family matters and entrust them to God, the Kingdom of Heaven invades our homes.

When we release obsession over our children and pour our heart into God, God’s sovereign reign begins over their lives.

When we surrender our business and career problems to God and confess our poverty of heart, God Himself guides and takes responsibility for our paths with His divine wisdom and power.

Declare complete default toward the world today.

Do not squander your heart on the empty affairs of this world, but possess God in His fullness.

I pray in the name of the Lord that all of you may richly enjoy each day this glorious present Kingdom of Heaven freely given to the poor in spirit.

[Prayer]

Living Heavenly Father, Sovereign Ruler of the universe and our very breath,

We repent before the cross of our foolish ‘wealth of heart,’ wherein we squandered our soul on worldly success, riches, and anxiety while excusing ourselves before You that we had no room in our hearts.

Lord, at this hour, we lay down all secular pride and attachment, confessing with tears that we are spiritual beggars who cannot live a single moment without Your grace.

Like Mary who broke her alabaster flask, grant us the strength to resolutely declare default toward worldly anxieties and temptations, pouring out our entire heart solely upon You.

May our faith not linger as a vague expectation of the afterlife, but may we daily possess and enjoy the Kingdom of God and His holy reign in our homes, workplaces, and every sphere of our lives.

In the precious name of our Lord Jesus Christ, who brought us the true blessing of the Kingdom of Heaven, we pray. Amen.
